## ✅ Shipped Aug 9

**The four permanent labels.** Verification (`Checked directly` / `Official source` /
`Needs checking`) · `From the Field Guide` · `Sponsor` · `Built by Werth Design`.
A reader must never have to work out which of the four they're looking at, so each
gets its own word in its own position. The product module now carries its label
explicitly.

**[/advertising/](https://delawarefieldguide.com/advertising/)** — the independence
policy, written with nothing sold and nobody to disappoint. States what money cannot
buy, that a business gets its listing corrected whether or not it pays, and that the
no-commerce-near-safety rule is enforced in code rather than in judgment.

**Footer, three groups** — The Field Guide · From the Field Guide · Made here — with
the model in one sentence beneath. Nine links in a flat row made all three parts of
the business look like the same kind of thing.

**About → "Built here"** — a maker's mark. No price, no CTA, no *"need a website?"*.
Says outright that the relationship buys nothing: a studio client in a listing is
held to the same standard as anyone, and being a client is never a reason to be
covered.

## The rules, kept where they can't be forgotten

1. Never place commerce above a page's answer.
2. Never inside legal rules, emergency information, conservation warnings, safety
   information, fee explanations or verification disclosures. *(Enforced:
   `SAFETY_PAGES` + build gate.)*
3. At most one primary product module per editorial page. *(Enforced: `markFor()`.)*
4. Related products at the bottom.
5. Product availability never affects editorial inclusion or ranking.
6. **Do not create editorial pages to create product inventory.**

### Never
Shop link in the hero · Werth Design banners on editorial pages · popups · fake
urgency · sponsor modules that resemble editorial tags · SEO-heavy Werth anchors
through the publication · thin commercial articles · pay-to-play rankings.

## Why the studio relationship works this way

A reader finds the Field Guide because it answers a real question. They experience
structured data, filtering, search, maps, source tracking, SEO architecture,
natural-language Ask and a growing local information system — and *then*, if they
ever need a site built, Werth Design isn't an unknown agency. It's the studio behind
the Delaware site they actually use.

That proof is worth more than a banner, and it only works if the banner never appears.
